# Branch: Spatial reach and volume conduction

> **Open when** the learner asks why neighboring electrodes correlate, how far an electrode "sees," or whether you can work out where a signal came from. Most natural from Modules 7 or 8. **Return to the module you came from.**

## Guiding question

If every current source contributes a little to the potential everywhere, what does it mean for an electrode to record "a" cell?

## Ideas to explore

- volume conduction: the field from each source spreads through the conductive medium and adds to the fields of other sources;
- spatial reach: nearby sources dominate, but many distant synchronous sources can add up to a visible signal;
- why nearby sites correlate: they sample overlapping fields;
- source localization is an **inverse problem**: many different source arrangements can produce the same pattern at the electrodes, so you need extra assumptions or constraints to get a unique answer.

## Visual moments

- `widgets/extracellular_field.html`: compare traces at two nearby sites with traces at two distant sites, and turn on cell 2 to see superposition.
- Optional on-the-fly widget: two different source arrangements that produce nearly identical potentials at a sparse set of electrodes (a demonstration of nonuniqueness).
