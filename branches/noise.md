# Branch: Noise

> **Open when** the learner asks why a channel is noisy, what sets the noise floor, or whether lower impedance means lower noise. Most natural from Modules 7, 8, 10, or 11. **Return to the module you came from.**

## Guiding question

When a trace is noisy, where could that fluctuation have entered the chain between the cell and the file?

## Ideas to explore

- thermal noise: resistive elements produce random voltage fluctuations even with no signal;
- environmental pickup: line-frequency interference, nearby equipment, cabling, and poor shielding or grounding;
- amplifier noise: the electronics contribute their own noise floor;
- biological background: the summed activity of many distant cells is "noise" for detecting one nearby cell, but signal for other questions;
- **impedance and noise are related but not interchangeable.** A higher-impedance interface can contribute more noise and pick up more interference, but a single impedance number does not predict the noise on a channel. Make the learner say what else matters.

## Visual moments

- Optional on-the-fly widget: a spike waveform plus adjustable noise sources (broadband, line-frequency, biological background), with a detection threshold. Ask how each source affects what gets detected.
