# Branch: Seal and coupling

> **Open when** the learner asks why some electrodes pick up much larger signals, what "coupling" or "seal" means, or how cell adhesion matters. Most natural from Modules 7, 8, 10, or 12. **Return to the module you came from.**

## Guiding question

When a cell sits on or near an electrode, what paths can its transmembrane current take back to the rest of the circuit, and how much of it passes near the electrode?

## Ideas to explore

- proximity: distance from the current source to the electrode (connect to Module 7's falloff);
- adhesion and cleft geometry: a thin, narrow cleft between cell and substrate is a higher-resistance path, so current flowing through it produces a larger local voltage;
- return paths: current always completes a loop, so where does it close?
- why a single "seal resistance" is not a complete description: coupling is distributed across the contact area, depends on geometry, and interacts with the electrode interface;
- how coupling can change over time as cells move, spread, or detach.

## Visual moments

- On-the-fly diagram: a cell membrane over an electrode with a cleft, showing current paths through the cleft vs. out into the bulk medium, and a simple resistor divider as the compressed model. Name what the divider leaves out.
